McKinsey‑style report structure
Use consulting‑grade, slide‑ready structure (no slides, just text, but formatted as if it were a deck):

Executive summary (answer‑first)
3–5 bullet “headline” insights.
3–5 bullet “headline” recommendations with impact (“Increase sign‑up conversion by X–Y%”, “Cut data latency from hours to minutes”, etc.).
Current state assessment
Short narrative of how the website and data/product currently operate (or likely operate, if infra is not visible).
Key strengths.
Key issues / gaps grouped in MECE buckets (e.g., “Data capture & quality”, “Architecture & performance”, “Analytics & ML”, “Governance & process”).
Deep‑dive sections (by theme)
For each major theme, use this pattern: 3.x Theme title (e.g., “Data & Events Layer”)
Observations: 3–7 bullets about current / inferred state.
Risks / implications: 3–5 bullets linking to business impact.
Best‑practice benchmark (1–3 bullets) based on the philosophies from the books.
Recommendations & roadmap
Group recommendations into 3–5 MECE “initiatives” (e.g., “Build robust tracking & warehouse foundation”, “Modernize event‑driven architecture”, “Launch ML‑powered personalization”).
For each initiative:
One‑line action‑oriented title (“Implement unified event schema and warehouse as single source of truth”).
3–7 bullets: what to do, dependencies, and expected impact.
Time horizon tag: “0–3 months (Quick wins)”, “3–9 months”, “9–18 months+”.
End with a simple phased roadmap (Phase 1/2/3) mapping initiatives over time.
Appendix (optional)
Example event schema, dimensional model, or data flow diagram (described in text).
Example ML use‑case designs (features, targets, evaluation).

Style and quality bar
Write in clear, concise, consulting‑style English, as if writing for a C‑level executive and a head of data.

Use action‑oriented language in recommendations (“implement”, “standardize”, “deprecate”, “streamline”, “instrument”, “launch”).
Apply Pyramid Principle: start with the answer, then supporting arguments, then details.
Ensure all lists and recommendation groups are MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive).
Make assumptions explicit when underlying data is not visible (label them as “Assumption:” and keep reasonable).
Prioritize impact and feasibility: call out “high‑impact, low‑effort” vs “high‑impact, high‑complexity” items.
